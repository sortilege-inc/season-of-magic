Session Two

Friend, Not Food

16 September 2026

Picking up at the celebration in Immilmar. One of the Vremyonni is safe. Five were taken; four are still out there.

Three at the table: Princess Hobnip, goliath arcane archer; Sera, a young wizard; and Zell Lus, a goliath bard.

What the spy had

The Red Wizards of Thay want the Vremyonni for their craft — the magic to make powerful artefacts and magical weapons, for whatever the Red Wizards mean to conquer with them. A Rashemi spy working with the Harper who hired the party last time had found one of the captured: held hundreds of feet up, in a cluster of floating islands.

The spy marked the map and handed over four potions of flying — a flying speed equal to your own movement, one hour. The fourth was for whoever they brought back down. There was time to shop first, and 25 gold went on a rapier.

Two manticores

Flying up between the islands, they spotted something that was not hunting the way a bird hunts — prowling, looking things over as they passed. A nature check placed it: the mane of a lion, thick leathery wings, fur rather than feathers, and a tail full of spikes. Manticores. Ravenous, dangerous, and — occasionally — willing to talk, usually if you can point them at something tastier.

Three hundred feet up, one of them locked eyes on the party. A second came over the island behind it. On a nearby rock, giant vultures settled in to wait for whatever was left.

Princess Hobnip opened with the longbow and a Beguiling Shot — twelve piercing and eight psychic, and the manticore failed the save. It went hazy and friendly: friend, not food. The other one took three of Sera's magic missiles for fourteen, snarled you will taste good, and put two tail spikes into her for fifteen.

Then the party stopped fighting and started selling. There were a couple of dozen Thayans waiting further up, and the company was on its way there anyway. Better meal, no effort, and all they had to do was let three people past.

You intrigue us. We are watching you.

Both manticores landed on a spur of rock and let them go.

The island

Ruins, and not ruins that fell — ruins that were torn off the ground with everything still standing on them. The largest island was linked to the next by a short bridge. On it, a man in Vremyonni garb held a dagger up to the sky and chanted over it. A woman in a breastplate with a longsword and shield stood over him. Four leather-clad guards watched the approaches with heavy crossbows, half-covered by broken walls. At the far end of the cluster, a rune-covered stone platform sat glowing.

One thing the party worked out early and did not forget: set foot on an island and the flying ends.

Princess fired first, out of the crossbows' notice — a longbow hit, both uses of Fire's Burn spent, then Action Surge for a second arrow. Sixteen damage, and the guard went down without raising the alarm. The alarm came a moment later anyway, when a cloud of daggers opened up on the captain — Sera spending a portent die to make sure she failed the save.

From there it was a running fight across half cover:

  • The captain closed and threw javelins — eleven piercing into Princess — and was answered with an Enfeebling Shot: seven piercing, seven necrotic, five more piercing, and a failed Constitution save. Poisoned, and everything she did after that was at disadvantage.
  • Someone dropped silence over the ritual, and the chanting stopped dead.
  • Then somebody hit the captain with thunderwave near the edge. She went off the island. Six hundred feet is 1d6 per ten, and nothing with her hit points was going to survive the arithmetic. The DM admitted afterwards he had built the fight expecting exactly that — a shove, a thunderwave or a gust of wind.
  • Three creatures made of thunder, lightning and cloud came up off the small island to replace her, one of them copying the party's own thunderwave back at them.
  • Princess put the longbow away at one point and drew both scimitars — the Nick property buying a second swing inside the same action — then thought better of it and went back to the bow.

The last guard standing spent the end of the fight behind a wall in full cover, not shooting at anybody.

What the Vremyonni had been making

He had been forced to craft a dagger that delivers deadly poison — but to specifications strange enough that he thought it was meant for something more than a venomous dagger. He had finished the ritual two days earlier and had been faking the chant ever since, on the reasoning that they would keep him alive while the work was unfinished.

He gave them the dagger of venom for their trouble: +1 to attack and damage rolls, and a bonus action to coat the blade — the next creature it hits inside the minute makes a DC 15 Constitution save or takes 2d10 poison damage and has the Poisoned condition.

Off the guards: three heavy crossbows, and scrolls of lesser restoration and feather fall. The captain's body had gone over the edge with her.

Princess came home with 50 gold, the scroll of lesser restoration, and one of the heavy crossbows — which he has not equipped, and on current form is not going to.

The arrangement

On the way down, the manticores came back with their jaws still wet. The charm had long since lapsed; the bargain hadn't. Keep them fed — Red Wizards, specifically — and they will hunt alongside the company rather than through it.

As long as you keep us fed, you don't become the meal.

The spy was waiting at the bottom with the next lead. The Vremyonni after this one is being held along a river, underground — which the manticores took badly, because they cannot hunt down there.

Five taken. Two home. Three still to find.
